## About the class

Follow cattle nutrition from digestion and dry matter to hay analysis, water and minerals, life stages, supplementation, and winter feeding. Connects with [Agronomy Fundamentals](../agronomy-fundamentals/README.md) and [Pasture and Grazing Fundamentals](../pasture-and-grazing-fundamentals/README.md).

Cedar Run Farm and its feed values and ration calculations are fictional teaching examples, not a ration prescription for a real herd. The class focuses on cattle; nutrient needs and mineral suitability differ between species. Supplied PDFs are unchanged.

## Lesson 1: How Cattle Digest Forage

Understand the four stomach compartments, rumination, and the microbes that turn forage into usable nutrients. Connect forage structure and feed changes with rumen function.

## Lesson 2: Dry Matter and Feed Intake

Compare pasture, hay, and supplements on a dry-matter basis, distinguishing feed offered, feed consumed, and inventory used. Includes worked intake and feeding-loss calculations.

## Lesson 3: Energy, Protein, and Fiber

Interpret energy, crude protein, and fiber measures on a cattle feed report, and explore how forage quality and intake together affect nutrient supply.

## Lesson 4: Reading a Hay Test

Define and sample a hay lot, interpret as-fed and dry-matter report values, and compare hay purchases by dry matter and estimated energy supplied.

## Lesson 5: Water, Minerals, and Vitamins

Explore dependable water access, mineral delivery, vitamin sources, and species differences. Includes a worked example of tracking mineral disappearance.

## Lesson 6: Feeding Cattle at Different Life Stages

Relate maintenance, growth, pregnancy, and lactation to feeding needs. Use body condition and feeding records to investigate changes and plan feeding groups.

## Lesson 7: Supplementation and Ration Arithmetic

Identify a nutrient shortfall, calculate a possible supplement contribution, account for displaced forage, and compare costs within the whole diet.

## Lesson 8: A Winter Feeding Plan for Cedar Run

Bring hay inventory, nutrient supply, cold-weather conditions, feeding access, and spring turnout together in a fictional farm’s winter feeding case.
